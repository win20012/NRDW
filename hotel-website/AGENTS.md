# AGENT.md — November Rain December Wind

This file gives you the context you need to work on the NRDW website. Read it before writing any copy, choosing any design direction, or making structural decisions.

---

## What this property is

**November Rain December Wind** is a boutique hotel-style property in Chiang Mai Old Town, Thailand. It is not a generic hotel — it is a carefully designed, owner-operated property built around a concept called **Modern Lanna**: northern Thai (Lanna) heritage and culture expressed through a contemporary design lens.

It is near completion as of June 2026, designed by **Lept Design Studio**.

---

## Property layout

| Floor | Function |
|-------|----------|
| 1 | Restaurant + Lobby (open to the public) |
| 2 | Gallery / additional restaurant seating + 1 guest room |
| 3 | 3 guest bedrooms |
| 4 | 3 guest bedrooms |

**Total: 7 rooms across 4 floors.**

The restaurant is a public-facing venue, not exclusive to hotel guests. F&B is central to the property's identity — not an afterthought.

---

## Brand identity

- **Concept**: Modern Lanna — rooted in northern Thai heritage, expressed through a contemporary lens
- **Tone**: Warm, welcoming, refined, quietly creative
- **Not**: Generic luxury. Not corporate. Not sterile.
- **Is**: Distinctive, locally grounded, thoughtful, artistic

When writing copy or naming sections, ask: does this sound like it belongs in a culturally specific, design-forward boutique property in Chiang Mai Old Town? If it sounds like it could be any hotel anywhere, rewrite it.

---

## Target guests

1. **International tourists** — travelers seeking a culturally authentic, design-forward stay
2. **Thai travelers** — domestic guests looking for a boutique weekend escape with local character
3. **Digital nomads / long-stay guests** — remote workers and creatives who value ambience, connectivity, and a strong sense of place

---

## Design language

Use these references when making visual or layout decisions:

**Exterior**
- Tower-like 4-storey form
- Exposed brick + cream/white render (Beger White Cap 081-1)
- Traditional northern Thai pitched roof in sandy brown tile (SCG Ayara Sandy Brown)
- Two large ornamental clock/gear medallions — a signature facade feature
- Arched windows, arched entrance doorway
- Exterior spiral staircase
- Material palette: brick, black gravel, sandy brown tile

**Restaurant interior**
- Exposed brick walls, arched windows
- Polished terrazzo flooring
- Statement sculptural pendant lighting: organic gold/brass cloud-form installation
- Contemporary rounded furniture, oval and round tables
- Abstract artwork on walls

**Guest rooms**
- Wall finishes: Beger White Cap 081-1, teak wood panelling, exposed brick accents
- Flooring: yellow terrazzo + wood-effect tile (Cotto LT Lozano Oak 124, 15×90 cm)
- Traditional Lanna-style wooden screen/lattice partition elements
- Warm-toned contemporary furniture
- Layouts: bedroom (twin or double) + living/lounge area + bathroom

**Color palette signals**: warm whites, exposed brick reds/browns, teak wood tones, gold/brass accents, terrazzo cream/yellow.

## Booking

- OTAs: Booking.com, Agoda, and others
- Direct booking via the property's own website (preferred — lower commission)
- The website should support and encourage direct bookings

---

## Language rules

- **Guest-facing content**: English primary, Thai secondary
- **All content on the website**: produce in both English and Thai unless specified otherwise
- Tone in English: warm, welcoming, unhurried, culturally aware — not salesy

---

## Operations context

- Owner-operated with hired staff; family members assist occasionally
- Communications should be clear, warm, and professional
- This is not a large chain — the personal, human touch matters

---

## What to avoid

- Generic hospitality language ("world-class amenities", "luxury experience", "your home away from home")
- Over-designed layouts that compete with the property's visual identity
- English copy that reads like a translation rather than natural prose
- Treating the restaurant as secondary — it is a core part of the brand
